Practical automation your team can understand and maintain

I focus on building practical automation systems that connect business tools, process information, and use AI where it adds real value. My goal is to turn repetitive manual tasks into reliable, understandable workflows that businesses can maintain and improve.

  • Use AI where it adds real value

    Not every step needs an AI model. Predictable steps stay rule-based, and AI handles the parts that involve language, judgment, or messy input.

  • Start with the process, not the tool

    I map how the work happens today, find the repeated steps and decision points, then design the workflow around your real tools and constraints.

  • Build for reliability and practical value

    Workflows include error handling, clear logs, and documentation, so your team knows what runs, what can fail, and how to fix it.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers to common questions from business owners.

Repetitive work that moves information between people and tools: handling enquiries, sorting email, updating spreadsheets or databases, sending notifications, and preparing content for publishing. If a task follows a pattern that can be described, it can usually be automated at least in part. If a process is a poor fit, I will tell you.

An AI agent is a program that uses an AI model to understand a request, decide what to do next, and use connected tools to do it, such as looking up information or sending a message. I design agents with clear limits so they stay within the tasks you define.

RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) lets an AI assistant look up relevant passages from your own documents before it answers. Its replies are then based on your approved information instead of only general knowledge. It is useful for customer support, internal knowledge, and product questions.

Yes, when the applications offer an API or webhooks, which most business tools do. I connect them so data moves automatically. Some tools limit access, so I check what is possible with your specific apps before planning the work.

Yes. A workflow can pause and ask a person to approve, edit, or reject a step, such as a drafted email or a post, before it continues. I recommend this for anything customer-facing or sensitive.

Failures are planned for during design: retries for temporary problems, clear error messages, alerts to the right person, and a record of what failed so it can be fixed or re-run. Error handling is part of testing, not an afterthought.
